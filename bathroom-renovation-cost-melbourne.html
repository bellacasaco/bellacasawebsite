PRICE RANGES

Typical bathroom renovation costs in Melbourne.

There is no single price for a bathroom renovation, because no two bathrooms are the same. A compact ensuite with a like-for-like layout is a very different job from a large family bathroom with a freestanding bath, a walk-in shower and custom joinery. What we can give you is a realistic picture of where the market sits and where a premium renovation lands.

The ranges below are indicative guides for a full renovation (strip-out, plumbing, waterproofing, tiling, fixtures and finishes), not quotes. Your real price depends on your room and your selections, which is why we assess every home in person before we put a number in writing.

COST DRIVERS

What changes the cost of a bathroom renovation.

Two bathrooms of the same size can differ by tens of thousands of dollars. These are the factors that account for most of the difference.

◆

Size and layout

A bigger room needs more tile, more waterproofing and more labour, and moving the shower, toilet or bath adds plumbing and floor work.

◆

Tile choice

Tile is both a materials and a labour cost. Large-format tiles reduce grout lines but need flatter surfaces and careful setting, and marble or natural stone raises the price of both the tile and the installation.

◆

Waterproofing and prep

Wet areas should be waterproofed to AS 3740 and left to cure before tiling. In older homes, uneven floors and worn substrates add preparation time. This is never the place to save money.

◆

Shower and glass

Frameless glass costs more than a framed screen, and a walk-in or wet room needs carefully set falls and drainage. Linear drains, ceiling rain heads and twin shower heads add to fixture and plumbing cost.

◆

Vanity and joinery

A standard vanity is the budget option. A custom floating or curved timber vanity with a stone top, plus lit mirrors and recessed niches, is a premium one.

◆

Tapware and fixtures

Fixtures vary the most from one project to the next. Brushed brass, matte black and designer tapware cost more than standard chrome, as do wall-hung toilets and freestanding baths.

◆

Electrical and ventilation

Lighting, heated towel rails, heat lamps and extraction fans need a licensed electrician and may involve new circuits or ducting.

◆

Age and access

Older homes can reveal surprises once walls and floors are opened, and tight access or an upper floor adds time. A clear process for handling those surprises matters as much as the price.

WHAT YOU PAY FOR

Where the money goes.

A full bathroom renovation budget usually covers the same set of items. Knowing them makes quotes easier to compare, because a low price often means one of these has been left out.

  • ◆Strip-out and removal of the old fixtures, tiles and waste.
  • ◆Plumbing and drainage, including moving or replacing pipes and fitting off tapware.
  • ◆Waterproofing of the floor and walls in wet areas, to the Australian Standard.
  • ◆Tiling, both the tile itself and the labour to set it, which is often one of the largest single items.
  • ◆Vanity, mirror and joinery, from a standard unit to a fully custom piece.
  • ◆Shower screen and glass, framed, semi-frameless or fully frameless.
  • ◆Tapware, fixtures and accessories, the most variable line in any budget.
  • ◆Electrical, lighting and ventilation, installed by a licensed electrician.
  • ◆Finishing, cleaning and project management, including a clean, tidy site and a handover walkthrough.
FIXED PRICE

Fixed price or estimate: why it matters.

An estimate is a guess that can grow. A fixed price is a commitment. When we quote, we assess your home in person, write down the full scope of work, and give you one number that covers it. It is not a low figure designed to grow once work begins.

Exceptions are rare but real. If something unexpected is uncovered once walls or floors are opened, we stop, show you exactly what we have found, and agree any cost impact with you before continuing. You are never billed for extra work without being told first.

When you compare quotes, make sure they cover the same scope. Check which tiles and fixtures are included, what is excluded, whether waterproofing and project management are covered, and whether prices include GST. See exactly what is included in our fixed price.

TIMELINE

How long does a bathroom renovation take?

A bathroom renovation can take anywhere from 2 to 3 weeks, depending on how big the renovation is. A compact ensuite with an unchanged layout sits towards the shorter end, and a larger bathroom with layout changes, a wet room floor or custom joinery sits towards the longer end.

Before work starts we give you a dated schedule, then manage the trades in sequence to keep to it. Choosing your tiles and fixtures early keeps your start date and schedule on track. See the full bathroom renovation process, step by step.

FAQ

Bathroom renovation costs, answered.

How much does a bathroom renovation cost in Melbourne? +

Most full bathroom renovations in Melbourne fall somewhere between $20,000 and $45,000, and high-end projects with custom joinery, natural stone or major layout changes can cost more. Our indicative starting price is $27,500 including GST ($25,000 + GST), but that is a base: your fixed price is confirmed after we assess your space, and it depends on the size of the room, the layout and your finishes.

How much does a luxury bathroom renovation cost? +

Luxury bathroom renovations in Melbourne commonly start in the $30,000s and can reach $50,000 or more, depending on size, materials such as natural stone or large-format marble-look tile, custom vanities, glass and lighting. The cost is driven by the number of premium elements and the complexity of the build, not just the room size.

What is the most expensive part of a bathroom renovation? +

Tiling (materials and labour) and plumbing are usually the largest items, with fixtures, tapware, custom joinery and frameless glass adding the most variation from one project to the next. Moving the shower, toilet or bath to a new position adds plumbing and floor work and is one of the quickest ways to increase the price.

Is a fixed-price quote really fixed? +

In the vast majority of cases, yes. We map out the full scope before quoting, so the price you agree is the price you pay at handover. The exception is if something unexpected is uncovered once walls or floors are opened. In that case we stop, show you what we have found and agree any cost impact with you before continuing.

How long does a bathroom renovation take? +

A bathroom renovation can take anywhere from 2 to 3 weeks, depending on how big the renovation is. We give you a dated schedule before work begins and manage the trades to keep to it.

What is a realistic budget for a bathroom renovation? +

For a full professional renovation in Melbourne, plan on roughly $20,000 to $45,000 depending on size, layout changes and finishes, and more for a high-end specification. The price bands earlier on this page show where each finish level sits, and your fixed price is confirmed after we assess your space.

Does the price include GST? +

When we quote a price we state clearly whether it includes GST. Our indicative starting price is $27,500 including GST, which is $25,000 plus GST, and it is a base rather than a final figure. Always check whether any price you are comparing is inclusive or exclusive of GST.
